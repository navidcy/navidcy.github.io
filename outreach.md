## Music

<div style="text-align:center; font-size:70%; margin-bottom:1.5em;"><img src="/img/dark-oceanography.jpg" alt="A percussionist's hand on a glass sphere resting among marbles during a performance of Dark Oceanography" style="width:100%; padding-bottom:0.5em;" /><br/>Photo: Darren Gill</div>

Together with percussionist [Louise Devenish][louise-site]{:target="_blank"}, composer [Kate Milligan][kate-site]{:target="_blank"}, and music technologist [Aaron Wyatt][aaron-site]{:target="_blank"}, we created [*Dark Oceanography*][dark-oceanography-site]{:target="_blank"}, a performance work that brings together climate science and experimental music.

*Dark Oceanography* is written for three percussionists and a multichannel spatial sound system. We followed the pathways of ocean eddies from the East Australian Current through the Southern Ocean and around the globe, using Lagrangian tracking data from eddy-resolving (0.1°) ACCESS-OM2 ocean model simulations, and turned these pathways into music that sonifies the impact of global warming on the ocean. The work was developed by [The Sound Collectors Lab][soundcollectors-site]{:target="_blank"} and the [ARC Centre of Excellence for 21st Century Weather][21cw-dark-oceanography]{:target="_blank"}, and premiered on 27 July 2025 at the David Li Sound Gallery, Monash University, performed by Louise Devenish, Niki Johnson, and Sofia Carbonara.

Read more about how we made it in [The Conversation][theconversation-dark-oceanography]{:target="_blank"}, or watch our [talk at the ACCESS Community Workshop 2025][access2025-dark-oceanography-video]{:target="_blank"}.

In August 2026, *Dark Oceanography* won **Work of the Year: Electroacoustic/Sound Art** at the [2026 Art Music Awards][artmusicawards2026]{:target="_blank"}, presented by APRA AMCOS and the Australian Music Centre.

<div style="text-align:center; font-size:70%; margin-bottom:1.5em;"><img src="/img/artmusic-award.jpg" alt="Receiving the Work of the Year: Electroacoustic/Sound Art award for Dark Oceanography at the 2026 Art Music Awards" style="width:80%; padding-bottom:0.5em;" /><br/>At the 2026 Art Music Awards in Melbourne</div>

## Public lectures

In 2024 I gave a public lecture, *Oceans in Motion: How Oceans Affect Our Changing Climate*, as part of the University of Melbourne's [July Lectures in Physics][july-lectures-site]{:target="_blank"}, a public lecture series that has been running since 1968. The talk is about how the ocean's currents and turbulent eddies move heat, carbon, and salt around the globe, and how they shape and regulate the climate.

## Radio, The Conversation, and other activities

On August 2018 me and Jeffrey Parker published a paper providing a potential explanation for 
mysterious the discoveries of _Juno_ regarding the depth-extent of the Jupiter's stripes. 
I summarised our findings in a piece at [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/jupiters-magnetic-fields-may-stop-its-wind-bands-from-going-deep-into-the-gas-giant-101324){:target="_blank"}. 
Our findings attracted lots of media attention and in the weeks that followed I gave a few 
radio interviews discussing them.
